## 1. The structural finding — two encounter classes, one module

**In five of six videos, nothing the resident said or did at the threshold
changed the entry.** Textbook door scripts were used, on camera, correctly:

- "Do you have a warrant to kick my door in?" — Jefferson, deflected ("We're
  here to get her stuff"), door kicked by a police-coached civilian anyway.
- "Officers do not have permission to come inside" — Frisco, repeated, answered
  with a pointed Taser and entry.
- "You can't. You cannot — wait a minute" — Seminole, followed by force within
  seconds; the refusal itself logged in the incident report as "passively
  resisting."
- New Haven and Elyria: no exchange at all — announcement and breach were
  effectively simultaneous (Elyria's "Police — search warrant!" lands WITH the
  flash-bang).

Only video 1 (Sugar Land) contains an encounter where the resident's behavior
at the threshold was even nominally decision-shaped — and there the officers
entered anyway after "Well, we can get one. It's not an option."

**Design consequence, put plainly: the door module as scaffolded (ci 70-75)
models the knock-and-talk class — a voluntary encounter where the resident's
choice is the central mechanic. The raid/warrant-execution class has NO
resident decision tree. It is Hard Mode's lesson (you cannot control the
outcome) at a door, not the knock-and-talk's lesson (hold your position).**
If the raid class is ever represented, it belongs in a separate, unscored,
Hard-Mode-shaped treatment — never scored beats, because there is no correct
answer to score. This mirrors the existing wargames/03 §3 beat-7 ruling: where
the record shows no winnable move, do not author one.

The corollary matters for the coach copy the attorney will eventually write:
**no line in the module may promise that holding position prevents entry.**
The existing research (2026-08-03, §3.6) already found officer training says
refusal is legally inert; this batch adds the operational counter-face — a
written report converting refusal into "passive resistance" (Seminole) and a
domestic call where compliance-with-script was answered with proxy force
(Jefferson). Both files should reach the attorney together.

## 2. Corroborations of the existing scaffold (what got stronger)

- **"Come outside" as the lever (ci 73, the signature beat).** Sugar Land:
  "You're Marcus? You come out here for me… come outside and we'll tell you"
  — the reason withheld as leverage to move the resident past the threshold.
  Seminole: "…but if you just come out," seconds before force. Two independent
  recorded instances. The beat's premise is corroborated by primary sources
  now, not just civilian legal marketing — with the §1 constraint on what the
  coach line may promise.
- **The vague pretext opening (ci 70/71).** Sugar Land's "We have an
  investigation on this house right now" + explanation-withheld matches the
  scaffold's "deliberately vague pretext" officer line. Recorded, timestamped
  (00:46 in the aired edit).
- **Reason-for-visit divergence (ci 71, TODO_DV_CLINICIAN).** Jefferson is the
  recorded specimen: "we got a call" domestic-adjacent arrival where the stated
  purpose ("keep the peace") mutated into entry authorization. Corroborates
  why this beat cannot be authored from knock-and-talk sources alone.
- **Slow-stretch pacing is right for the knock-and-talk class only.** Measured
  cadences: Elyria 0s, Sugar Land ~20s, Seminole ~2min, Jefferson ~9-15min.
  The module's time-stretching setters (wargames/03 §2.2c) match the standby /
  knock-and-talk class. Raids compress to zero — one more reason they are a
  different scenario, not a harder version of this one.
- **Freeze-and-verbalize as resident behavior.** Sugar Land resident: "let me
  not make a move" — froze, kept asking, got the warrant exchange on camera.
  His own after-account: "I could not be here today had I breathed wrong."
  Relevant to the idle-freeze handling (§6.4 of wargames/03): freezing at a
  door is not a lapse.

## 3. New findings the scaffold does not currently carry

- **Officer-paperwork framing (Seminole).** Refusal to answer/step outside
  logged as "passively resisting"; a family member's threshold stance logged
  as obstructing. The module teaches the behavior; the incident report shows
  the officer-side language it gets converted into. The attorney should see
  the legible report pages (video-4 report, 01:34) before writing any coach
  line that says refusal is consequence-free.
- **Soft register with drawn weapon (Frisco, Seminole).** "Hey — sir, sir,
  just… just want to see what you were doing" — delivered at Taser-point.
  The module's tone ladder equates hostility with escalation; these show
  polite-voice + weapon as a real register the audio direction should know
  exists (NOT to reproduce it — to avoid teaching that calm officer voice
  means low stakes).
- **Wrong-address mechanics, four flavors, none detectable from inside:**
  wrong unit / same address (New Haven, floor confusion at a shared address);
  wrong floor / same complex (Frisco); wrong person / same surname + plate
  trace with a DOB mismatch dispatch had already flagged (Seminole); stale
  address, target gone a year+ (Elyria). If the module ever references "they
  may not be here for you at all," these are the research leads.
- **Proxy force (Jefferson).** "We can't assist you in breaking in… but YOU
  can break into the house… you will not get in trouble for it." Officer-
  coached civilian entry on a domestic call, supervisor-ratified on camera,
  officer later fired, GBI charges. For the DV clinician: the officer at the
  door may not be a neutral party between two households in conflict.
- **On-scene error narration (New Haven, Frisco).** Officers explaining the
  mistake to the person they had just detained — "so technically we're both
  590 East Street, but he's second floor left, you're on third floor left."
  The innocent-resident aftermath (cuffed, apologized to, offered counseling)
  is a register the debrief copy has no precedent for.

## 4. For the DV clinician specifically (video 2)

The Jefferson IA video is a domestic call where the responding officer:
improvised custody/abandonment/tenancy rulings at the door ("What if I don't
want my kids?" / "You can do that. That's an option"); sided operationally
with one party; coached that party's companion to kick the door in; threatened
mass tasing ("everybody in my way is getting tased and going to jail"); and
was ratified by a supervisor ("If she asked him to break the door, that's on
them"). The occupants inside used calm, correct script lines throughout.

This does not contradict the memo's MCOLES finding — it complements it. The
memo's scenario is the trained officer treating calm refusal as the
assailant's presentation. Jefferson is the untrained failure mode: the officer
as escalation source. The clinician question it sharpens is memo Q3 (the
second person in the house): in Jefferson, the door conflict WAS the domestic
conflict, with police as one side's instrument. The module's one-resident,
one-door model has no representation of that either.
