### 1b. Findings

**F1 — CRITICAL — Three different stories about the same 48 states.** `/pack` L89 (ES block) tells a Colorado driver "This state's own laws are still being reviewed"; `/rights/co/` says "We have not verified Colorado's own rules yet… not shown"; `/rehearse?state=CO` shows eight Colorado lines "checked against the statute text". A reader who touches two surfaces meets a contradiction. Fix: one sentence, generated from `data/hud.json` `review` + `notice`, used by all three (pack `s_pending_t`, build-pages gap note, HUD notice). Effort M.

**F2 — CRITICAL — `/how-we-verify` L390-395 is violated by the shipping HUD.** It says LIKELY is "never published" and Unverified "never published in any form", and "three states are published". `data/hud.json` ships 423 lines for 51 states: 258 VERIFIED, 117 UNASSESSED, 32 NULL, 8 REFUTED, 6 LIKELY, 2 CASE_LAW_ONLY. 24 of the non-VERIFIED lines are **state-specific claims** (posture not universal), e.g. NV/MN/AR `firearm/onRequest/UNASSESSED` "If asked, say whether you are carrying a firearm" (a duty asserted with no cite and no verification), OH `silence/refusal_offense_limited/UNASSESSED` "you must give your name, address and date of birth (R.C. §2921.29)", IA `lying_is_crime/REFUTED`, IN/NH `sign/REFUTED`, VA/PA/VT/WA cannabis lines UNASSESSED. Only LIKELY lines get a visible flag; UNASSESSED/NULL/REFUTED render identically to VERIFIED minus the cite (`model.ts` L54, `arena/index.html` L2162). Fix: either (a) filter the HUD compiler (`tools/jurisdictions/hud.mjs`) so a state-specific posture with a non-VERIFIED verdict falls back to the universal sentence, or (b) rewrite `/how-we-verify` to describe what actually ships. (a) is the one consistent with every other surface. Effort S for the filter (one predicate), M to regenerate + re-check. The research docs agree with (a): `research/state-matrix.md` L6 "Shippable states: 0", `findings-summary.md` L3 "NOTHING HERE IS SHIPPED TO USERS" — both now false.

**F3 — HIGH — `/how-we-verify` L397 overclaims the source check.** "re-fetches every primary statute page behind a published rule": `research/law-watch.json` watches **four** URLs (TX §521.025, TX §543.005, NY §1194, GA §40-5-29). ## 2. The rights-pages opportunity

**Correction to the brief's framing.** 48 *states* lack verified rules, but only **24 states = 48 pages** are noindexed (`build-pages.mjs` L211: `noindex = !isVerified && !aid`). The other 24 unverified states (AL, AZ, CA, DC, FL, IL, IN, KY, LA, MD, MA, MI, MN, MS, MT, NC, OH, OR, PA, SC, TN, VA, WA, WI) are already indexed and in the sitemap because they carry a `STATE_LEGAL_AID` entry — they show the same federal floor plus one directory link. Noindexed: AK, AR, CO, CT, DE, HI, ID, IA, KS, ME, MO, NE, NV, NH, NJ, NM, ND, OK, RI, SD, UT, VT, WV, WY.

**What `data/hud.json` has for those 24.** Counting only VERIFIED-verdict lines whose posture is state-specific (not `universal/none/unassessed/myth`): 18 of 24 have ≥2 such lines (AK, CO, CT, DE, HI, KS, ME, MO, NE, NV, NJ, NM, ND, OK, SD, UT, WV, WY); all 24 have ≥1; typical page would gain 2-4 cited, state-specific sentences (sign-citation posture, firearm duty, unmarked-car rule, sometimes lying-is-crime, footage retention). For comparison TX/GA/NY have 3-4 such lines in the HUD. So the substance is real but thin: 2-4 sentences, not the 6-7 quoted rules a verified page has.

**What it would take in `tools/build-pages.mjs`** (currently reads only `pack.html` literals, L166-172):
1. `readFile(data/hud.json)`; per state select `lines.filter(l => l.verdict === 'VERIFIED' && STATE_SPECIFIC(l.posture))` (must exclude the 24 non-VERIFIED state-specific lines from F2 or the page contradicts `/how-we-verify`).
2. Render a "What changes in {State}" block **above** the federal floor: `.note` with `hud.ui.provisional[lang]` + `st.notice[lang]`, then the lines with `withoutInlineCite` (as `model.ts` L49) and the cite on its own line. Do not reuse `.stq` — that class means "quoted statute text" and these are paraphrases.
3. `noindex = !isVerified && !aid && stateLines.length < 2`; `ogSlug` set when indexable.
4. `tools/render-og.mjs` L64-72 needs a third `kind` — the 'verified' badge says "QUOTED FROM THE STATE STATUTE" (false here) and 'federal' says "FEDERAL RULES APPLY HERE" (understates). Add "CHECKED AGAINST THE STATUTE · NOT ATTORNEY-REVIEWED".
5. FAQ JSON-LD (L199-204) gets the extra lines; hub page (L316-317) needs a third list ("Provisional state lines"); sitemap +36 to +48 URLs; feed +18-24 items.
6. Prerequisites in the data, or the ES pages ship broken: 5 ES lines contain raw English (F-ES1), 17 states' ES notices use English state names (F-ES2), 12 EN notices say "a Alaska-licensed" (F-ES3) — all fixed in `tools/jurisdictions/hud.mjs` templates, one place.
7. Copy that must change the same day: `/how-we-verify` L390-395, `/about` L355/357, hub L309, the charter comment in `build-pages.mjs` L19-35 ("An unverified page never states a state-specific rule"), the gap note L243 ("not shown for {State}").
8. No tests exist for `build-pages.mjs` (only `build-jurisdictions.test.mts`); add one `node --test` on the selection predicate.

Effort: **M** (~100 lines across two tools + two copy pages + hud.mjs template fixes; 1-2 days). **L** if the owner wants one shared selection module for `model.ts`, `arena/index.html` L2146-2148 and build-pages, which today triplicate `ORDER`/`EMPH`.

**SEO upside.** 48 pages flip from noindex to indexable, each already titled "Your rights at a traffic stop in {State} — Amparo" with reciprocal hreflang, a distinct OG image once rendered, and the hub/feed links. Today those pages earn nothing. Search volume per state is **UNVERIFIED** (no keyword data in the repo); the states are mostly small (WY, ND, SD, VT, NH, ME, DE, RI, AK, HI, ID, KS, NE, WV, IA, AR, MT-excluded) with NJ, CO, NV, MO, OK, CT, UT, NM as the ones that matter, and NJ/CO/NV/NM/UT/CT are also the Spanish-speaking-population ones — where the ES pages cannot rank until the state names are Spanish (F-ES2). Realistic expectation: long-tail, high-intent, low-volume; the differentiator versus ACLU-affiliate and law-firm pages is the cite-level specificity, which is exactly what the page would now carry.

**Legal-posture risk, against the generator's own rule.** `build-pages.mjs` L13-17/L27: every published rule is "lifted verbatim… quoted, with citations"; L33-35: "An unverified page never states a state-specific rule." The HUD lines are neither verbatim nor quoted; they are compiled sentences with a cite (`hud.mjs`). `/how-we-verify` L390: a rule is published only at **Verified** (quoted from an official site or primary text). A VERIFIED-verdict HUD line meets that bar's *substance* (the cell was read against primary text per `RESEARCH-STATUS.md`) but not its *form* (quotation). The provisional notice discloses bar (b) — no attorney — and does so more clearly than the existing three pages; it does **not** address the paraphrase-vs-quote gap, and it does nothing for the 24 non-VERIFIED state-specific lines, which the rule forbids outright. Two more facts the owner should weigh: the pages' cites would be unwatched (F3 — 4 URLs watched), so the footer's "sources last checked" line would be false on every new page unless changed; and the repo's own research header (`state-matrix.md` L6, `RESEARCH-STATUS.md` "Zero states are shippable", `findings-summary.md` L16-17 citing *Upsolve, Inc. v. James* as gating "regardless") says none of this should ship — yet the Arena/app HUD already ships it for 51 states. Whether that was an explicit decision is **UNVERIFIED** (not found in the files audited). I am not assessing *Upsolve*; I am noting the repo cites it against itself.

**Does the notice satisfy the rule?** Only after the rule is rewritten. As written, no: the rule is "quoted, verified, or not stated". With (1) VERIFIED-only filtering, (2) a rewritten `/how-we-verify` that names a second tier ("checked against the statute, paraphrased, cited, not attorney-reviewed, sources not yet watched"), and (3) the notice above the block, the pages would be at least as honest as the HUD that already ships — the same claim on a crawlable URL, not a new category of claim.

**Recommendation (owner decides).** Do it, gated as above, and do the four prerequisites first (F2 filter, F-ES1-3, F3 footer, `/how-we-verify` rewrite) — they are needed anyway because the HUD already makes these claims. The tradeoff: +48 indexable pages on the highest-intent query in the category, in exchange for moving the crawlable surface from "3 states of quoted statute" to "27 states of paraphrased statute" with 4 watched sources and no attorney, and a correction burden that scales with every cell. The lazy alternative that costs nothing in posture: on the 48 pages, replace "not shown for {State}" with a link to `/rehearse?state=XX` ("this state's provisional lines are in the Arena") — no charter change, no new crawlable claim, no SEO gain.

**Legal terms, ruling used below:** *registro* for a vehicle search is the term ACLU/legal-aid Spanish materials use and is understood in MX/CA; keep it, but a Mexican driver would say *revisión* and *cateo* is the term for a warrant search of premises — so the **door** drill should say *orden de cateo*, not *orden*. *Multa* is the fine, not the ticket; the document you sign is *la infracción / el citatorio* (MX) or *la citación* (US legal Spanish); "firmar la multa" reads as agreeing to pay. *Oficial* (police) / *agente* (Border Patrol) is used consistently across surfaces and both are natural vocatives in MX — no change.
