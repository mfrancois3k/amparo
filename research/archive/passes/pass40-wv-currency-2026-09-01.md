## WEST VIRGINIA WALL — PARTIALLY CLEARED

`code.wvlegislature.gov` still refuses direct fetch. But `law.justia.com/codes/west-virginia/`
serves fully via direct fetch — the "host-blocked" designation in earlier passes applied only
to the official host, and Justia was never actually tested with a plain fetch (only through the
browser tool, which may have hit a different failure). This unblocks WV going forward for any
column still needing it.

**W.Va. Code §62-1A-10/-11 ("Motor Vehicle Searches" / "Rules for Certain Evidence of Consent
to Vehicle Search")** — verified in full body. An officer who stops a vehicle for a traffic
misdemeanor may not search it unless: (1) probable cause or another lawful basis exists; (2)
written consent is obtained on a compliant form; or (3) oral consent is obtained and captured on
a compliant audio recording. Effective 1 January 2011.

**And then the statute takes its own teeth out.** (d): "Failure to comply with the provisions of
this section shall not, standing alone, constitute proof that any consent to search was
involuntary." (f): "Nothing contained in this section shall be construed to create a private
cause of action." A court may also waive the recording requirement entirely on a finding that
recording it endangered officer safety (c), and a court finding of voluntary verbal consent
independently satisfies the section regardless of form or recording (e).

**This is a fifth remedy tier, weaker than anything found so far.** PASS 38 established tiers 0-3
(exclusion surviving consent / plain exclusion / "a factor" / none named). West Virginia goes
one further: the statute doesn't just omit a remedy, it **affirmatively disclaims** one. A form
and a recording requirement exist on paper, and the statute itself says noncompliance proves
nothing and creates no cause of action. Recorded as **Tier 4 — explicitly disclaimed.**

**Checkpoint authority: confirmed a real negative, not a host block.** The full section list of
ch. 17C art. 5 (Serious Traffic Offenses — WV's DUI article) was read in full. Zero occurrences
of "checkpoint," "roadblock," or "sobriety." The "operational guidelines / written notice to the
commissioner" language attributed to WV in secondary sources — already traced in an earlier pass
to the syllabus of *Carte v. Cline*, not to statute — is now doubly unsupported: neither the
official host nor Justia's mirror of the DUI article contains it.
