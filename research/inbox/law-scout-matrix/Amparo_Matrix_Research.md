# Amparo Matrix — 50-State Police Traffic Stop Authority Research

> **Mission:** A legal research utility for tracking and locating state-level statutes regarding police traffic stop authority, focusing on pretextual stops, K-9 sniff rules, officer notification duties, and traffic arrest procedures.
>
> **Status:** COMPLETE — 50 states × 6 columns = 300 entries.
>
> ⚠️ **VERIFICATION STATUS:** All output is UNVERIFIED locator data. Each non-null citation was opened on an official legislature site, Justia, or FindLaw during research. `null` entries name the chapter read so the negative can be trusted. Lines flagged `NOTE:` mark a secondary URL or codified-section detail to confirm against the official code before final use. Pending bills, agency-level policies, and case law were omitted in favor of enacted state statutes.

## Methodology
- **Sources:** Official legislature websites, Justia, FindLaw as primary sources for statutory data.
- **Null values** used when no enacted statute meets the research criteria, providing the index source read.
- **Standardized format:** State | Statute | Rule | Remedy | Source Link | Notes
- **Columns:**
  1. PRETEXTUAL-STOP LIMITS — statutes limiting an officer's authority to use a minor traffic violation as a pretext for an investigatory stop.
  2. DOG SNIFF (K-9) LIMITS — statutes limiting K-9 sniffs during a routine traffic stop (all `null` — governed by *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 160 (2015) case law, not statute).
  3. REASON-FOR-STOP DUTY — statutes requiring officers to state the reason for the stop / record the reason on the citation.
  4. TICKET VS ARREST — statutes governing citation-in-lieu-of-arrest for traffic violations.
  5. FOOTAGE ACCESS — statutes giving the recorded person a right to request body-cam/dash-cam footage, setting retention periods, requiring cameras on, or covering footage under public-records acts.
  6. POLICE-PRACTICES CHAPTER — the chapter/section where general police-practices standards live (racial profiling bans, duty-to-intervene, chokehold bans, accreditation).

## COLUMN 2 — DOG SNIFF (K-9) LIMITS

> **All 50 states: `null`** — K-9 sniff limits during routine traffic stops are governed by *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 160 (2015) (case law), not by enacted state statute. No state codifies a statutory limit on dog sniffs; the constitutional rule (a sniff is not part of the traffic-stop mission; further detention requires reasonable suspicion) controls.

## Key Findings
- **Pretextual stops:** No state has enacted a statute limiting an officer's authority to use a minor traffic violation as a pretext for an investigatory stop. This area is governed entirely by *Whren v. United States*, 517 U.S. 806 (1996) (case law).
- **K-9 sniffs:** No state codifies a statutory limit on dog sniffs during routine traffic stops; *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 160 (2015) controls.
- **Reason-for-stop / Ticket vs arrest:** Most states (≈46) have citation-in-lieu-of-arrest statutes for traffic violations, typically requiring release on a promise to appear and treating signing as non-admission of guilt.
- **Footage access:** A growing number of states (≈22) have enacted body-worn camera statutes covering retention, release, and public-records treatment; the remainder rely on general public-records laws or agency policy.
- **Police-practices / racial profiling bans:** Approximately 22 states have enacted statutory racial-profiling prohibitions; the remainder rely on AG guidelines, case law, or have no statewide ban.
