UT | Sign citation | null (Utah Code 77-7-18 and 77-7-20, verified via le.utah.gov -- 77-7-18 governs who may ISSUE a citation (peace officer, public official, port-of-entry agent, animal control officer, authorized volunteer); 77-7-20 governs the officer's duty to give the citation to the individual and electronically file it with the court within 5 business days. Neither section imposes any duty on the driver to SIGN a citation.)
UT | License (driver ID) | VERIFIED Utah Code 53-3-217 -- Licensee shall have license certificate in immediate possession while driving and shall display it upon demand of a justice of peace, peace officer, or division field deputy/inspector; violation is an infraction, defense if valid license produced in court.
NH | Ticket vs arrest | REFUTED RSA 594:14 "Summons Instead of Arrest" (Title LIX, Ch. 594) IS an enacted statute directly on point -- officer with probable cause of misdemeanor/violation may issue written summons in lieu of arrest, with statutory form. NH R. Crim. P. Rule 3(d) (not Rule 4) is a companion court rule that cross-references and requires the summons be "in the form required by statute. See RSA 594:14." So citation-in-lieu-of-arrest lives in BOTH a statute (RSA 594:14, the primary authority) and a court rule (Rule 3(d), procedural cross-reference) -- it is not rule-only.
NH | Duty to intervene | CONFIRMED RSA 106-L:20 "Law Enforcement Agencies; Duty to Report an Allegation..." -- imposes a duty on the chief executive officer of a law enforcement agency to REPORT misconduct (valid complaint, sustained finding, or resignation under investigation) to the Law Enforcement Conduct Review Committee within 15 business days. It does not impose any duty on an individual officer to INTERVENE in another officer's misconduct.
TN | Checkpoint authority | REFUTED Tenn. Code 55-10-413 is "Additional Fees" (ignition interlock fee, treatment fund fee, BAC test fee added to DUI convictions under Sec. 55-10-401) -- has nothing to do with checkpoints; there is no subsection (f) matching a checkpoint cite. Title 55, ch. 10, part 4 covers DUI offense/testing/implied-consent (401-430) but contains no statute expressly authorizing sobriety checkpoints; Tennessee checkpoint stops rest on implied-consent/testing provisions plus general law enforcement authority, not a dedicated checkpoint statute.
TX | Checkpoint authority | VERIFIED Texas has NO enacted statutory sobriety-checkpoint authority -- HB 439 (82R, 2011), which would have added CCP Chapter 65 authorizing DPS/large-county/large-city checkpoints, died in committee (left pending 4/12/2011) and was never enacted; SB1700 (introduced 82R/88R references) likewise concerned license/insurance checkpoints and was not enacted either. Texas courts (State v. Holt) have found checkpoints unconstitutional under Tex. Const. art. I, sec. 9 absent statutory authorization.
AL | Lit-place / safe stop | REFUTED Ala. Code 13A-10-53 "Defense" -- affirmative defense is that the arrest was unlawful OR that the driver, aware of the officer's stop signal, "stopped his or her vehicle within a reasonable time and at a reasonable location based on the facts and circumstances of the stop." This is a generic reasonable-time/reasonable-location defense, not a safe/lit-place-specific defense.
AK | Lit-place / safe stop | CONFIRMED Alaska Stat. 28.35.182(c) -- affirmative defense to failure-to-stop is that the peace officer's vehicle lacked required lighting/audible signals and proper markings, OR the officer (if not in a vehicle) was not wearing uniform/displaying badge so as to be "reasonably identifiable as a peace officer." This is an officer-identifiability defense, not a lit-place/safe-stop defense -- confirmed distinct from AL 13A-10-53.
