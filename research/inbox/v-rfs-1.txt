CA | Reason-for-stop duty | VERIFIED Cal. Veh. Code 2806.5(a)-(c) -- officer must STATE (verbally) reason for stop before questioning, and document it on citation/report; exception if withholding needed to protect life/property (terrorism, kidnapping) [remedy: none stated]
CO | Reason-for-stop duty | REFUTED C.R.S. 42-4-1709(5) -- not a spoken-reason duty; governs tender of a penalty-assessment notice to a defendant who refuses to accept service (constitutes service upon tender)
CT | Reason-for-stop duty | null (read Public Act No. 23-95 (Sub. SB 1022), approved June 26, 2023, Sec. 1 (NEW): "Prior to the completion of any stop of a motor vehicle, a police officer...shall verbally inform the operator of such vehicle of the purpose for the stop." Enacted as an uncodified new section, not an amendment to CGS 54-1m. Checked CGS Title 54 Ch. 959 (Alvin Penn Act, contains 54-1l through 54-1t) in the 2025 and 2026 versions and Title 14 Ch. 248/249 vehicle chapters -- no CGS section number for this duty appears in any of them. No remedy-for-breach language exists in the Public Act text itself.)
IL | Reason-for-stop duty | REFUTED 625 ILCS 5/11-205 -- titled "Public officers and employees to obey Act -- Exceptions"; governs authorized-emergency-vehicle privileges (may proceed past red signals, exceed speed limits, etc. when responding to calls), not any duty to state a stop's reason
MN | Reason-for-stop duty | VERIFIED Minn. Stat. 169.905 -- peace officer making a traffic stop must inform the operator of a reason for the stop unless unreasonable under the totality of circumstances; officer may not ask the operator to guess the reason [remedy: officer's failure to comply "must not serve as the basis for exclusion of evidence or dismissal of a charge or citation" -- suppression expressly barred]
KY | Reason-for-stop duty | REFUTED KRS 523.110 -- "Giving peace officer false identifying information," a Class B misdemeanor for giving a false name/address/DOB after warning; not a reason-for-stop duty (mis-filed as suspected)
NM | Duty to inform (firearm) | REFUTED N.M. Stat. 29-19-9 -- titled "Possession of license"; requires a concealed-handgun licensee to have the license in his possession while carrying, not to proactively inform an officer he is armed
OR | Duty to inform (firearm) | REFUTED ORS 166.380 -- "Examination of firearm by peace officer; presentation of concealed handgun license"; lets an officer examine a firearm carried in/on a public building (licensee may present the CHL instead), not a duty for the carrier to volunteer that he is armed
RI | Duty to inform (firearm) | null (confirmed the negative: read the full catchline index of Title 11 Ch. 11-47, all sections 11-47-1 through 11-47-64, plus full text of 11-47-8 "License or permit required for carrying pistol" and 11-47-11 "License or permit to carry concealed pistol or revolver" -- no section imposes any duty, proactive or on-request, to inform an officer that one is armed. §11-47-58 is "Firearms -- State preemption," not a duty-to-inform section as a prior web source claimed.)
NM | Recording own stop | REFUTED N.M. Stat. 30-12-1 -- "Interference with communications; exception"; text covers displacing/cutting/tapping telegraph-telephone lines and reading/intercepting telegraph or telephone messages without consent, with an exception (subsection (3)) for a person who is a party to the communication or has consent of a party -- this is a wire/telephone interception statute, not a general recording-consent law, and by its own terms does not reach in-person conversation. New Mexico has no separate all-party-consent statute for in-person audio recording; recording one's own in-person traffic stop is not regulated by any NM consent-to-record provision (30-12-1's party-consent exception is the closest analog and only applies to telegraph/telephone interception).
