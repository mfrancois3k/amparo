OK | Police-practices chapter | VERIFIED 22 Okla. Stat. 34.3 -- "Racial profiling prohibited": no officer shall engage in racial profiling; race/ethnicity cannot be sole factor for probable cause/reasonable suspicion; agencies must also adopt written policy [DIRECT officer prohibition, plus separate agency-policy mandate] [remedy: violation is a misdemeanor (criminal); agency must take disciplinary action if officer violated agency policy]
OR | Police-practices chapter | VERIFIED ORS 181A.410 -- DPSST/board must set training, certification, and an educational program to reduce profiling by officers; no stop/search prohibition in this section [agency/training-body standards mandate, not a direct officer prohibition] [remedy: none stated -- only certification/training infrastructure, no discipline or suppression clause here]
TN | Police-profiling chapter | VERIFIED Tenn. Code 38-1-503 -- each law enforcement agency "shall adopt a written policy that prohibits racial profiling by its employees," due by Jan 1, 2016 [agency-policy mandate, not a direct statutory prohibition on the officer -- the ban exists only if/as written into the agency's own policy] [remedy: none stated -- no penalty, suppression, or civil action specified for a violation]
TX | Police-practices chapter | VERIFIED Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 2.132 -- requires each agency to adopt detailed written policy defining/prohibiting racial profiling, complaint process, data collection, and annual reporting to TCOLE [agency-policy mandate -- officers are bound only via the agency's required policy, not directly by this article] [remedy: agency corrective action against officer required by the policy; TCOLE must open disciplinary proceedings against chief administrator who intentionally fails to report -- but data "shall not constitute prima facie evidence of racial profiling" (subsec. c), limiting its use]. NOTE: this article was repealed by Acts 2023, 88th Leg., ch. 765, Sec. 3.001(1), eff. Jan 1, 2025 -- no longer current law as of today.
VT | Police-practices chapter | REFUTED 3 V.S.A. 168 -- this is NOT a profiling ban. Catchline: "Racial Disparities in the Criminal and Juvenile Justice System Advisory Panel." It creates a 16-member advisory panel housed in the AG's office that studies racial disparities and, per subsec. (f)(6)(B), reports to the legislature recommendations on "whether and how to prohibit racial profiling" -- i.e. the statute discusses the possibility of a future ban, it does not itself enact one. Same conflation pattern as the sister-batch findings: citing a study/advisory-panel statute as if it were the prohibition.
VA | Police-practices chapter | VERIFIED Va. Code 52-30.2 (Va. Community Policing Act, ch. 6.1) -- "No State Police officer shall engage in bias-based profiling in the performance of his official duties" (subsec. A); also mandates statewide stop-data collection (race, ethnicity, age, gender, reason, outcome, force used) reported to Community Policing Reporting Database, plus agency collection of excessive-force complaint counts [DIRECT officer prohibition (State Police only, per text), paired with a separate universal data-collection mandate on state and local agencies] [remedy: none stated in this section -- no suppression/discipline/civil-action clause attached to a bias-based-profiling violation itself]
WA | Police-practices chapter | VERIFIED RCW 43.101.410 -- local agencies "shall comply with" WASPC recommendations: adopt written policy condemning profiling, audit practices, train officers, maintain complaint review process, collect demographic stop data [agency-policy/process mandate -- no clause directly forbidding an officer from profiling; officers are bound only through the agency's own disciplinary procedures] [remedy: "officers found to be engaged in racial profiling must be held accountable through the appropriate disciplinary procedures within each department" -- discipline only, no suppression or civil action created]
WV | Police-practices chapter | VERIFIED W. Va. Code 30-29-10 -- "No law-enforcement officer shall engage in racial profiling" (subsec. c); agencies must also adopt policies/procedures (complaint investigation, discipline, training) to eliminate profiling [DIRECT officer prohibition, plus a parallel agency-policy mandate in the same section] [remedy: none stated for the direct prohibition itself -- subsec. (d) requires agencies to have "procedures to discipline" officers, but no statutory penalty, suppression, or civil action is created by the section]
WI | Police-practices chapter | REFUTED Wis. Stat. 165.85 -- this is NOT a racial-profiling provision at all. Catchline: "Law enforcement standards board." Full text confirmed (definitions, LESB findings/policy, officer/instructor/agency definitions) contains zero mention of "profil" anywhere in the section -- it is the general statute creating and defining the state's law-enforcement training/certification board, unrelated to racial profiling or bias-based policing.
