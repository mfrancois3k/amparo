## Do I have to sign the ticket? (Texas)

**Drill:** `?sit=last30` · **State:** TX

### English

```
In Texas, yes — and refusing is one of the few traffic offenses you can be arrested for.

Get a ticket? Sign it. Signing is only a promise to appear, not an admission of guilt (§543.005) — once you sign, the officer must promptly release you. Refusing to sign is one of the few traffic offenses you can be arrested for.

“To secure release, the person arrested must make a written promise to appear in court by signing the written notice.” — Transp. Code §543.005
```

## I was the passenger — do I have to give my name? (Texas)

**Drill:** `?sit=pass` · **State:** TX

### English

```
It’s not the same rule as the driver’s, and it’s not the same in every state.

You are not required to answer questions beyond identifying yourself when lawfully arrested (§38.02); passengers generally do not have to identify themselves unless arrested. But giving a FALSE name, address, or birthdate while lawfully detained is itself a crime (§38.02(b)).

“A person commits an offense if he intentionally refuses to give his name, residence address, or date of birth to a peace officer who has lawfully arrested the person.” — Penal Code §38.02(a)
```

## Can I refuse a breath test? (Texas)

**Drill:** `?sit=last30` · **State:** TX

### English

```
Silence covers questions — it does not cover chemical tests, and the consequences are separate.

Silence covers questions — not chemical tests. Refusing a breath/blood test after a DWI arrest triggers a license suspension (you have 15 days to request the hearing that contests it) — and police can still get a warrant for your blood. Roadside balance/eye tests before arrest are voluntary in Texas.
```
